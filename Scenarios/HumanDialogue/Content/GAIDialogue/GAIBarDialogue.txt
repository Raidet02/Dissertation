I tell you, if I scrub this counter one more time, I'll wear the wood down to the nails. Not that anyone's left to spill ale on it. 
No, we keep it clean for ghosts and expectations.
Used to be a place like this earned its mess. Muddy boots, loud laughter, a broken mug now and then. Now? Silence so thick you
could pour it. And still-still-it has to look "presentable."
...Presentable?
That's the word they used, yes. As if polish could make people stay, or make promises lighter to carry. Sweep the floor, mend the 
stools, smile like tomorrow's already paid for.
I've watched good folk leave at dawn with empty carts and fuller sense than most. Can't blame them. If I didn't have one last keg 
and a door to lock, I'd have gone with them.
You will soon?
Soon enough. Soon is the only mercy left around here. Finish your drink, dear. Places like this shouldn't be empty... but they 
shouldn't be kept waiting either.
Of course, some people love an empty tavern. No noise, no complaints-just tidy tables and the comforting illusion that everything 
is exactly as it should be.
They ride through, nodding at clean windows and swept streets, thinking it's all a testament to their fine stewardship. Never 
wondering why no one's inside to thank them.
They don't ask?
Why would they? Questions spoil the view. Much easier to admire from a distance and let others mind the cost.
Finish up. Wouldn't want our unseen guest to think we lacked manners.
